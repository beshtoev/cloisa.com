The problem: one client, two tax systems, two packages, every year

  • ~861,000

    US citizens estimated to live in Canada. A US citizen above the filing threshold files a 1040 wherever they live.1

  • US$10,000

    Penalty per missed US information form, such as Form 5471 or 8938. The statute of limitations stays open until the form is filed.2

  • US$1,745–2,745

    Published starting fee per year for a coordinated T1 + 1040 file at cross-border firms.3

A cross-border file is two returns that must agree.

A US citizen living in Canada usually has to file in both countries. A Canadian with US income often does too. None of these filings is hard on its own. The hard part: one client needs many of them at once, prepared by hand, every year. The table below shows what a single client can set off.

General purpose AI could draft much of this. But a tax file is full of names, Social Insurance Numbers, Social Security Numbers and account numbers, and the firm answers for every one of them. Typing client records into a public AI chatbot is exactly what a firm cannot do. And those chatbots were trained on the open internet, not on two tax systems whose rules change every year.

So the work is still done by hand. And every filing season, it eats the team's evenings.

02

The solution: Cloisa drafts, your tax professional approves

Four steps. Cloisa runs the first three on its own and lines the file up, complete, for review. You go through it when it suits your day. The file waits for you, not the other way around.

  1. Read

    Cloisa reads the client's documents as you have them: slips, statements and prior returns from both countries. No manual entry.

  2. Map

    Each figure placed on the T1 and the 1040, traced to the page it came from.

  3. Reconcile

    Each treatment checked under both tax codes and the treaty, with the rule it relied on quoted next to the figure.

  4. Approve

    The licensed professional reviews field by field and signs. Never the AI agent.

04

Built to grow your practice, not to replace it

Cloisa cannot sign a return, and it never will. The judgment stays with the professional. What changes is what your hours go to.

  • Serve more clients with the team you have

    The hours in a coordinated file go to slip-matching, credit sequencing and PFIC checks. When that work arrives drafted and cited, the same professionals handle more files, without hunting for cross-border staff that is hard to hire.

  • Say yes to the work you refer away

    A client moves to the US, or a US citizen walks in. For most practices that work is too rare to keep the expertise current, so the client gets referred out. With the mechanics drafted and every treatment cited, you keep the client and the relationship.

  • Reach people the current cost excludes

    Many cross-border filers go unserved because preparing the coordinated file costs more than they can pay. When drafting takes less of the firm's time, that work becomes worth taking, and a market that was out of reach opens up.

The goal, stated plainly: the practices that use Cloisa should serve more clients, offer a broader service, and earn more. We publish progress as it happens in Notes.
